R06 · bounded 2023 ecological context

A shared geography is not an individual transition.

Public 2023 estimates make a state-level pipeline comparison possible for ten states. The high-school measure is an annual flow of graduates; the higher-education measure is an enrollment stock. The comparison can describe where scale is concentrated, but it cannot estimate college-going or policy effects.

Geographic overlap

The same large state contexts appear in both measures.

Texas, California, Florida, and New York are the leading four states in both published source sets, though California and Texas switch order. Together they account for 46.7% of the national annual high-school graduate flow and 50.4% of the national higher-education enrollment stock.

Annual high-school flow

53,000 graduates across the ten states, or 70.7% of the 75,000 national estimate.

Higher-ed enrollment stock

358,836 students across the same ten states, or 70.4% of the 510,000 national 2023 estimate.

Same four leaders

TX, CA, FL, and NY contribute 35,000 of the annual graduate flow and 257,125 of the higher-ed stock.

Scale is not conversion

Across the ten states, the stock is 3.9x to 9.4x the annual flow. That range is descriptive, not a transition probability.

Why a rate would be wrong

Equal annual flows can sit beside very different enrollment stocks.

Six states each have a rounded estimate of 3,000 undocumented high-school graduates. Their same-year undocumented higher-education enrollment estimates range from 11,675 to 27,672, a 2.37x spread. Higher-education enrollment contains multiple cohorts and continuing students; it is not the destination count for one graduating class.

  1. FlowThe high-school estimate represents students graduating in an annual period.
  2. StockThe higher-education estimate represents students enrolled at a point/period across multiple cohorts.
  3. No linkageThe public aggregates do not identify whether graduates later enrolled, where they enrolled, or whether they persisted.
  4. No causal policy testPolicy conditions are not time-aligned or modeled here, so this comparison cannot estimate policy effects.
  5. Rounded estimatesPublished high-school state values are rounded and may not reconcile exactly to the national total.
