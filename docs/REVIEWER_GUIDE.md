# Reviewer Guide

This repository is intended to be evaluated as a small recurring research product, not only as a coding exercise. A reviewer should be able to understand the research question, inspect the public result, trace a number to source data, and see how the product would be maintained when a new release appears.

Live publication: `https://joseph-th.github.io/higher_education_and_immigration_demo/`

## Reviewer path

1. Open `deliverables/research_brief.pdf`. It is the fastest reviewer-facing artifact: headline findings, analyst judgment, interpretation boundaries, and a compact source/QA trail in three pages.
2. Open `site/index.html` through a local static server or deployed Pages URL. Read the six longitudinal findings, the latest-release review, inspect the state explorer, and open the three analyst case studies.
3. Open `site/methodology-crosswalk.html`. This shows how the project distinguishes IPEDS U.S. nonresident data from immigrant-origin, SEVIS international, undocumented, high-school-pipeline, and refugee/asylee concepts before attempting a comparison.
4. Open `site/population-trends.html`, then `site/undocumented-pipeline.html`. R04 keeps distinct national populations and source vintages separate; R06 uses only same-year 2023 state aggregates and makes the stock-versus-annual-flow boundary explicit so the pipeline comparison cannot be read as a college-going or transition rate.
5. Open `site/research-operations.html`. This is the portfolio model for coordinating the three recurring products named in the vacancy: kickoff, versioned partner intake, method-change review, quantitative QA, discrepancy resolution, publication gating, and archival handoff.
6. Open `site/production-cycle.html`. This turns the shared partner workflow into seven explicit stage gates with entry conditions, required records, blockers, exit conditions, and retained evidence, while keeping all three products at a truthful pre-kickoff portfolio state. Download the blank live-cycle log to see the record a real kickoff would populate and validate.
7. Open `site/research-library.html`, then `site/source-update-triage.html`. The library shows what is maintained; the R07 routing page shows what kind of source change triggers a routine refresh, methodology review, research-question review, governance review, monitoring-only action, or archival treatment, with all 17 sources covered and no inferred internal deadlines.
8. Open `site/publication-handoff.html`. This is the final pre-publication control: 17 public claims/controls are tied to an authority, source, QA gate, disclosure, and publication status, including the fixed R01 baseline, claim-level institution holds, explicit R04 non-addability/vintage, R06 stock-versus-flow, R07 source-routing, R08 candidate-year QA carry-forward, and R09 medium-priority evidence-review controls.
9. Open `site/release-readiness.html`. This consolidates eight upstream controls and makes the decision split explicit: the current 2024 publication is `READY_WITH_CLAIM_LEVEL_HOLDS`, while candidate 2025 remains `HOLD_YEAR_ADVANCE` because only 2 of 4 required package assets are available.
10. Open `site/data-governance.html`. This shows the data-minimization boundary and exact public-release allowlist. The build rejects any file under `site/downloads/` or `site/data/` that is not explicitly approved.
11. Open `deliverables/ipeds_portfolio_analysis.xlsx`. Review the formula-driven executive summary, change the state lookup dropdown, and inspect the reconciliation/benchmark sheets.
12. Open `deliverables/management_briefing_deck.pptx`. Review the six-slide management story: KPI summary, national trend, state recovery, structural drivers, and interpretation/QA guardrails.
13. Open `site/sql-analysis.html`. Inspect the six read-only queries and their independent checks, then open `sql/portfolio_queries.sql` to see CTEs, joins, conditional aggregation, ranking, and window functions in executable form.
14. Open `site/ad-hoc-analysis.html`. Review the three constructed management questions and how each one is narrowed to the minimum useful calculation, tied to a source authority, reconciled, and returned as a short response without inventing a requester or deadline.
15. Open `site/state-recovery.html`. Review why the 33/18 state recovery split differs across states: each 2019-2024 result is reconciled by undergraduate/graduate change, institutional control, and institution continuity, with concentration among leading institution contributors kept separate from causal interpretation.
16. Open `site/undergraduate-shift.html`. Review the fixed R01 baseline before any 2025 Fall Enrollment result exists: undergraduate enrollment rose by 31,592 in 2023-2024, 45 of 51 states/DC increased, public institutions contributed 28,580, and the top ten institution gains represented 20.6% of gross positive institution movement. The page deliberately leaves persistence unresolved and states the next-release decision rule in advance.

## A concise project explanation

The project asks how U.S. nonresident fall enrollment changed across U.S. degree-granting institutions from 2015 through 2024. I built the series from official IPEDS complete-data files, audited the variables and aggregation codes across all ten years, restricted the data to a documented NCES/Digest universe, and created national, state, student-level, institutional-control, and institution-panel analyses. I then added structural QA, an independent NCES Digest benchmark, source hashes, downloadable outputs, and a static public brief. The project also includes an update playbook and release checker so it can operate as a recurring data product rather than a one-time analysis.

## Findings worth discussing

The strongest findings are not merely descriptive rankings:

- The 2015-2024 national increase came entirely from graduate enrollment. Graduate U.S. nonresident enrollment rose by 138,765 while undergraduate enrollment fell by 75,365.
- Recovery is geographically uneven. Thirty-three states/DC were at or above 2019 by 2024, while 18 remained below.
- The state follow-up shows that recovery is primarily graduate-led: 27 of the 33 recovered states/DC are graduate-led. California's -33,282 shortfall is instead undergraduate-driven, with undergraduate U.S. nonresident enrollment down 37,674 while graduate enrollment is up 4,392 from 2019.
- Private nonprofit institutions account for the major positive shift by control, adding 82,825 U.S. nonresident students over the decade. The follow-up concentration test finds that the top 10 positive institution contributors account for 34.5% of gross positive movement, with 65.5% occurring outside the top 10; institution-specific latest-release QA holds remain in force where applicable.
- Institution-panel accounting shows the +63,400 national increase was not created by adding more institutions to the analytic universe. Continuing institutions contributed +66,981 while universe entry and exit netted -3,581.
- The newest release has a different short-run composition: from 2023 to 2024 undergraduate U.S. nonresident enrollment increased by 31,592 while graduate enrollment decreased by 2,053, producing a net increase of 29,539. The fixed R01 baseline shows undergraduate growth in 45 of 51 states/DC, the widest state breadth in the 2015-2024 annual series; public institutions contributed +28,580, and the ten largest positive institution contributions account for only 20.6% of gross positive institution movement. Persistence remains a next-release question.
- Current IPEDS subgroup values differ slightly from the March 2026 Digest publication even though the overall enrollment universe is effectively identical. The project records that source-vintage difference rather than hiding it.

## Interpretation boundaries

The project deliberately avoids claims the data cannot support. IPEDS U.S. nonresident status is not a complete measure of international, immigrant-origin, undocumented, DACA, or foreign-born students. Fall Enrollment is a point-in-time snapshot. State values are based on institution location. UNITID matching helps with endpoint accounting but does not fully resolve mergers, splits, acquisitions, or reorganizations.


Live source/release checks:

```bash
python scripts/check_for_new_release.py
python scripts/check_research_library.py

```
