Similar labels do not mean the same population.

A source-selection guide for IPEDS U.S. nonresident, immigrant-origin, international, undocumented, high-school pipeline, and refugee/asylee research.

Compare definitionsDownload structured crosswalk

Decision rule

Compare only after definitions and denominators line up

The current project uses IPEDS because it provides a consistent annual institution-reported series. Other Portal populations often require survey estimation, legal-status modeling, or different administrative systems. Those sources can answer important questions, but they should not be merged into one series merely because all relate to immigration and higher education.

Population definitions

Six research concepts, six different measurement rules

Each card states what the source measures, what it excludes, and whether the current IPEDS analysis can support a direct comparison.

Primary analytic measure

IPEDS U.S. nonresident

Institution-reported administrative count

Current source / vintage
NCES IPEDS Fall Enrollment
Fall 2024 provisional
Who is included?
Students who are not U.S. citizens or nationals and are in the United States on a visa or temporary basis without the right to remain indefinitely
What is excluded or not identified?
DACA recipients, undocumented students, and other eligible noncitizens are not reported in this category
Best use
Annual institution/state/national enrollment monitoring
Comparison rule

Do not treat this as a count of all international, immigrant-origin, undocumented, refugee, or foreign-born students

Immigrant-origin students

Survey estimate

Current source / vintage
Presidents' Alliance / Migration Policy Institute using U.S. Census Bureau CPS October Supplement
2023 CPS used by 2026 report
Who is included?
First-generation immigrants plus second-generation immigrants; first generation includes naturalized citizens, permanent residents, refugees, asylees/asylum seekers, TPS recipients, undocumented people, and other domestic immigrant residents
What is excluded or not identified?
International students in the U.S. on nonimmigrant student visas for temporary study are excluded from the first-generation immigrant estimate
Best use
Measure domestic immigrant-origin participation in higher education
Comparison rule

This is a household-survey population estimate with a broader concept than IPEDS U.S. nonresident; the two series should not be added or substituted

International students

Administrative SEVIS record count

Current source / vintage
U.S. Department of Homeland Security SEVIS Data Mapping Tool via Higher Ed Immigration Portal
October 2025 Portal figures
Who is included?
Associates, bachelors, masters, and doctorate level international-student records at SEVIS-certified institutions
What is excluded or not identified?
Portal totals exclude vocational programs, primary/secondary school, flight school, and language training
Best use
Current international-student monitoring
Comparison rule

SEVIS, Open Doors, and IPEDS use different timing, coverage, and reporting concepts; counts are not directly interchangeable

Undocumented students in higher education

Modeled survey estimate

Current source / vintage
Presidents' Alliance / American Immigration Council using ACS microdata
2026 fact sheet; report-specific methodology controls exact vintage
Who is included?
ACS microdata are modeled to identify people likely to be undocumented and enrolled in postsecondary education; DACA-eligible students are estimated as a subset
What is excluded or not identified?
Potential international students are excluded using demographic, school-attendance, duration-of-stay, and work criteria
Best use
Estimate the scale and composition of undocumented college enrollment
Comparison rule

This is an estimated legal-status population, not an observed administrative category; do not compare one-for-one with IPEDS U.S. nonresident counts

Undocumented high school graduates

Modeled pipeline estimate

Current source / vintage
Presidents' Alliance / Migration Policy Institute using ACS plus NCES graduation rates
2019-early 2023 pooled ACS used by 2026 report
Who is included?
Undocumented high-school students under age 22 are estimated from ACS; differential graduation rates are then applied to estimate annual graduates
What is excluded or not identified?
This is not a cohort linkage to students later observed in higher education
Best use
Assess the potential higher-education access pipeline
Comparison rule

Do not interpret the annual graduate estimate as a transition rate into college or as the same population observed in higher-education enrollment data

Explicit measurement gap

Refugee/asylee students

Population embedded within broader immigrant-origin measures

Current source / vintage
Presidents' Alliance / Migration Policy Institute immigrant-origin framework
2023 CPS used by 2026 report
Who is included?
The first-generation immigrant category explicitly includes resettled refugees, asylees, and asylum seekers among other domestic immigrant groups
What is excluded or not identified?
The current project does not separately identify refugee/asylee students, and IPEDS U.S. nonresident is not a refugee-status measure
Best use
Identify a documented measurement gap before making refugee-specific claims
Comparison rule

A refugee/asylee estimate requires a source designed to identify that status; neither the current IPEDS series nor the aggregate immigrant-origin total isolates it

Review source methodology

Research implication

Use the right source for the question

Current longitudinal series

Use IPEDS U.S. nonresident when the question is annual institution/state/national enrollment under the IPEDS reporting definition.

Domestic immigrant-origin students

Use the CPS/MPI framework when the question concerns first- and second-generation domestic students rather than temporary-study visa holders.

Undocumented students

Use report-specific ACS-based estimates and preserve their modeled-estimate limitations instead of treating IPEDS visa categories as a proxy.

Refugee/asylee students

Do not make subgroup claims from this project. A population-specific source is needed because the current series does not identify refugee or asylum status.
