Research memo · Fall 2024

Enrollment recovered. The latest growth was undergraduate-led.

A concise research and publication-QA memo on U.S. nonresident enrollment at degree-granting U.S. institutions.

Fall 2024 provisional2015-202450 states + DC

Bottom line

Recovery is national. Its composition is not the same as before.

U.S. nonresident enrollment reached 1,044,137 in Fall 2024, or 5.31% of enrollment in the analytic universe. That is +63,400 students above 2015.

The newest annual movement breaks with the decade-level pattern. Undergraduate enrollment increased +31,592 in 45 of 51 states/DC, with public institutions contributing +28,580; graduate enrollment declined -2,053, producing a net +29,539. R01 remains a fixed baseline: call the shift broadly persistent only if the next complete release is positive nationally and in at least 26 of 51 states/DC.

Three findings that matter

01

The recovery became graduate-heavy

Graduate enrollment is +138,765 above 2015; undergraduate enrollment is -75,365 below it.

02

State recovery remains uneven

33 states/DC are at or above 2019; 18 remain below. FL led the latest annual increase at +6,207.

03

Institution-universe change does not explain the gain

Continuing institutions contribute +66,981 over 2015-2024, while universe entry/exit nets to -3,581.

Analyst judgment

A flag does not automatically become a finding

The same QA process can lead to three different publication decisions. These examples are drawn from the current evidence-backed review log.

Hold the anomaly

Community College of Rhode Island

+1,176 students

IPEDS jumps from 12 to 1,188 U.S. nonresident students between Fall 2023 and Fall 2024. CCRI's official Institutional Effectiveness enrollment dashboard independently reports total enrollment of 12,518 in Fall 2023 and 12,275 in Fall 2024, exactly matching the project IPEDS totals for both years. CCRI's official international-student admissions page separately defines an international student as a foreign national studying on an F-1 non-immigrant temporary visa and explicitly excludes U.S. citizens, permanent residents, eligible non-immigrant visa holders, refugees, asylees, and temporary protected status populations. That F-1-only admissions construct is narrower than the project IPEDS U.S. nonresident category, and the enrollment dashboard does not expose a directly comparable nonresident-status series. The exact all-student totals therefore corroborate the institutional frame while the explicit visa definition explains why CCRI's public international-student surface cannot validate the 12-to-1,188 nonresident jump. Preserve IPEDS in aggregates and keep institution-specific interpretation held pending a directly comparable residency/citizenship measure.

Corroborate the direction

Collin County Community College District

+2,256 students

IPEDS rises from 433 U.S. nonresident students in Fall 2023 to 2,689 in Fall 2024 (+2,256). Collin's FY2025 ACFR reports Fall headcount of 37,776 in Fall 2023 and 39,552 in Fall 2024, within four of IPEDS totals, and the Foreign category rising from 612 to 2,482. Its 2023-24 CDS reports 604 International (nonresident) students. Institutional labels are not assumed IPEDS-identical and counts are not substituted. Preserve IPEDS as numerical authority and clear only bounded direction-level discussion of the +2,256 movement.

Explain the structural change

The College of Saint Rose

-115 students

The institution leaves the 2024 analytic universe after reporting 115 U.S. nonresident students in 2023. New York State Education Department records The College of Saint Rose as closed on December 31, 2024, with academic instruction ending earlier in 2024. The universe exit is therefore an expected structural event rather than an unexplained data disappearance.

Interpretation boundary

What this memo can support

Population

IPEDS U.S. nonresident status is a federal reporting category. It is not a complete measure of immigrant-origin, undocumented, DACA, foreign-born, or all international students. The population crosswalk documents which companion source should answer each of those questions.

Use

The analysis supports enrollment monitoring and publication QA. It does not identify the causes of enrollment change or estimate immigration-policy effects.

Evidence

Official source archives, schema audits, arithmetic reconciliation, external NCES benchmarking, and institution-level evidence review are kept separate so each claim can be traced to its support.

Publication

Unresolved institution anomalies remain in official aggregate calculations but are withheld from institution-specific narrative until directly comparable evidence is available.

Methods and source trail

Primary measure

The series uses NCES/IPEDS Fall Enrollment EFNRALT for U.S. nonresident students. The analytic universe is degree-granting institutions in the First Look/Digest Title IV selection, located in the 50 states and District of Columbia.

Population comparability

IPEDS U.S. nonresident, SEVIS international-student records, CPS immigrant-origin estimates, and ACS-derived undocumented-student estimates answer different questions. The project maintains a separate population/source crosswalk before any companion comparison is attempted.

Release control

Historical revised IPEDS files are preferred when available. Fall 2024 is treated as provisional. Source URLs, archive sizes, retrieval records, and SHA-256 hashes are retained so later revisions can be distinguished from analysis errors.

Independent checks

All 24 NCES Digest consistency checks pass. State, institutional-control, and student-level aggregates reconcile to the national series, and institution entry/exit accounting is separated from continuing-institution change.

Publication QA

The latest-release queue contains 27 high-priority records. 27 high-priority records have evidence-backed manual dispositions; unresolved cases remain in official aggregate calculations but are withheld from institution-specific claims.

Use boundary

This is an enrollment-monitoring and research-QA product. It does not estimate causal policy effects and it does not convert one immigration-related population definition into another.
